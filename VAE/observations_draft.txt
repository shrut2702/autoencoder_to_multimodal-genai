- when the real data lies between 0 and 1 use bernoulli distribution for maximizing the data likelihood
- when the real data is continuous and not bounded use gaussian distribution for maximizing the likelihood of real data, this approach is probabilistic. On the other we can also use MSE loss for minimizing the difference between decoded data and real data, this approach is deterministic and doesn't focus on increasing the overall likelihood of the real data.

gaussian dist experiments:
- the mnist data normalized around 0, probabilistic decoder with per-pixel or per-data mean conditioned on z and per-pixel variance conditioned on z without restricting or clamping the log variance the training becomes unstable and decoder produces noisy reconstructed image
- the mnist data normalized around 0, probabilistic decoder with per-pixel or per-data mean conditioned on z and per-pixel variance conditioned on z with restriction on log variance by clamping values between -4 and 4, the training becomes more stable than earlier, model learns and improves slightly, both encoder and decoder improves, decoder producing slightly improved reconstucted images which looked like model is learning the real data distibution (but digits not clearly visible), but loss becomes stagnant near -250 and model stops improving (possibly because of hard clamping the log variance values), resulting into generating visually comprehensible images but blurry and noisy
- the mnist data normalized around 0, probabilistic decoder with per-pixel or per-data mean conditioned on z and single global learnable variance not conditioned on z, the training is most stable among this three experiments, the loss becomes stagnant near 300, based on test set's latent vector's distibution it can obsereved that encoded learnt to producde latent vectors normlaized around 0 with variance near 1 across each latent dimension, the decoded images are much more visibly comprehensible and digits can be seen but little noisy
- the cifar-10 data normalized around 0, probabilistic decoder with per-pixel or per-data mean conditioned on z and single global learnable variance not conditioned on z, but here n latent vectors are sampled from each data's posterior distribution (encoder) instead of 1 and further n likelihood distributions based on n latent vectors are produced and log likelikhood of the data is maximized across these likelihood distrbutions, the loss is converging till 2600 after which it becomes stagnant, the sampled image from the decoder's distribution is too much noisy, the kl divergence is fluctuating and starts from high value only, based on tSNE the latent vectors for each category are not clustered together but instead spread out everywhere (based on the image observed in each category, even though they belong to same category they varies very much from one another, here CNN encoder might help instead of linear layer) and the latent vectors are normalized around 0 with variance nearly 1 across each dimension, even though latent vectors are distributed normally the encoder hasn't learnt to compress image into informative latent vectors

bernoulli dist experiments:
- the mnist data 0-1, decoder that produces bernoulli distrbution and here the per-pixel mean of the bernoulli distribution is treated as reconstructed data instead of sampling from that distribution, the training is most stable with kl divergence under appropriate range and becoming stable after 20000 steps, the digits in reconstructed images are clearly visible and not blurry. here, the latent vectors are loosely clustered compared to gaussian decoder VAE and also the latent vectors are not normalized around 0 (doesn't match prior), even though the reconstructed image from the real data has good quality, but because of poor probabilistic encoder when the latent vectors are interpolated for the same image and decoded the images produced are doesn't match that image
- the cifar data 0-1, decoder that produces bernoulli distrbution and here the per-pixel mean of the bernoulli distribution is treated as reconstructed data instead of sampling from that distribution, but here n latent vectors are sampled from each data's posterior distribution (encoder) instead of 1 and further n likelihood distributions based on n latent vectors are produced, the training is stable with kl divergence under appropriate value, the images reconstructed by decoder are better and less noisier than the one sampled from gaussian VAE decoder but still they are blurry, and the loss becomes stagnant after reaching 1850, based on tSNE here also the latent vectors from each category are not clustered (as mentioned above the reason might be highly varying images - background, texture, colors -- CNN encoder might be able to capture such relationships), the latent vectors across all categories and dimensions are spread normally around 0 with variance near 1

conclusion: in case of CIFAR data, a CNN encoder might improve the encoding distribution and further may inprove reconstruction quality of images making them more clearly visible rather than producing blurry images, but since the MNIST has much simpler images and very much similar across each digit, the MLP probabilistic encoder clusters the latent vectors across categories in much better way and therefore the interpolated latent vectors when decoded reconstructs semantic images unlike with the CIFAR dataset
